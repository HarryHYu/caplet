# Risk and trade-offs

## What you will learn

- Explain what is given up when one option is chosen.
- Separate a possible gain from a guaranteed gain.
- Explain why spreading exposure cannot remove every risk.

## Choosing means giving something up

A **trade-off** occurs when gaining one thing means accepting less of another. An **opportunity cost** is the value of the next best alternative given up. It is not the sum of every possible alternative.

Moneysmart's teaching resource explains opportunity cost through choices between uses of limited money and time. ([Moneysmart](https://static.moneysmart.gov.au/teaching/bite-size/Bite-size-Yr7-10_What-is-opportunity-cost.pdf))

Imagine a school business group choosing how to use its afternoon. Preparing products leaves less time to advertise. Advertising leaves less time to prepare products. Even without a payment changing hands, time has another possible use.

The best alternative depends on the purpose of the decision. One group may need to finish orders already promised. Another may need to find customers. Identifying opportunity cost does not choose for them. It makes what they give up easier to explain.

## Risk is about uncertain outcomes

**Risk** is the possibility that an outcome differs from what is expected, including losing money. A **return** is the gain or loss from an activity or investment. An **investment** uses money with the aim of receiving a future benefit.

A forecast describes what might happen. A recorded result describes what has happened. Mixing these two can make an uncertain plan appear safer than it is.

Moneysmart explains that higher possible returns generally come with greater risk of loss. Taking more risk does not guarantee earning more. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-to-invest/choose-your-investments))

It helps to separate two questions. How likely is an unfavourable outcome? How serious would it be? A relatively unlikely problem can still matter if it would leave essential costs unpaid. The size of the possible gain alone cannot answer those questions.

## Compare complete choices

A comparison can include the amount committed, the time required, possible costs and how soon money is needed again. A promise of a large return without explaining these features leaves the comparison incomplete.

In a classroom discussion, a group can describe a stronger sales outcome and a weaker one. The purpose is not to claim that both are equally likely. It is to notice which assumptions the decision depends on.

A choice can also have benefits that are not measured in dollars. Learning a skill or meeting a school deadline may matter. A money calculation can inform the discussion without representing every value in it.

## Spreading exposure

**Diversification** means spreading money across different investments to reduce dependence on any one of them. **Concentration** means having much of the exposure in one place. **Exposure** means how much an outcome can affect the person or business.

Different investments may react differently to events. That can reduce the effect of a single investment doing badly, but losses remain possible. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-to-invest/diversification))

Owning many things is not automatically a strong spread of risk. If they depend on the same customers or economic conditions, they may be affected together. The relevant question is what could cause each one to lose value.

## Worked example

*These numbers are not real. They only show the idea.*

A fictional school stall has $600 available. It chooses equipment instead of its next best alternative, a promotion campaign.

**Available money**

- Money held: $600
- Total available: **$600**

**Chosen use**

- Equipment purchase: $400
- Money kept for later costs: $200
- Total allocated: **$600**

The next best alternative was a $400 campaign. The opportunity cost is the benefit the group gives up from that campaign, not an extra $400 bill. Whether the equipment produces a better result remains uncertain.

## Things to think about

- What is the next best alternative, rather than every imaginable alternative?
- Which result is known, and which is only expected?
- Could apparently different investments be affected by the same event?

## Check your understanding

1. What is opportunity cost?
   - a) Every bill ever paid
   - b) The value of the next best alternative given up
   - c) A guaranteed investment gain
2. True or false: Higher risk guarantees a higher return.
3. Why can diversification reduce some risks without removing all risk?

## Answers

1. **b) The value of the next best alternative given up.** It concerns the alternative choice.
2. **False.** Greater possible gain can come with greater loss risk.
3. **Different investments may behave differently, but some events affect many together.** Spreading does not guarantee no losses.

## Glossary

- **Trade-off:** Giving up some of one thing to obtain another.
- **Opportunity cost:** Value of the next best alternative given up.
- **Risk:** Possibility of a different outcome, including loss.
- **Return:** Gain or loss from an activity or investment.
- **Investment:** Money used for a hoped-for future benefit.
- **Diversification:** Spreading money across different investments.
- **Concentration:** Much exposure in one place.
- **Exposure:** How much an outcome can affect someone.
