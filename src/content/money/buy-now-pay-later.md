# Buy now, pay later

## What you will learn

- Explain what happens when payment is delayed.
- Recognise costs and checks.
- Compare payment timing without choosing a product.

## Receiving an item before finishing payment

**Buy now pay later (BNPL)** is an arrangement that lets someone receive goods or services and pay over time. An **instalment** is one part of the total payment. **Credit** means receiving money, goods or services with an obligation to pay later.

ASIC, the Australian Securities and Investments Commission, is a government regulator for companies and financial services. ASIC explains that a BNPL provider pays a seller some or all of the purchase price. The customer then owes the provider under a separate agreement. ([ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/credit/buy-now-pay-later-credit-contracts-credit-licensing))

This creates two relationships: the purchase from the shop, and the payment agreement with the provider. The small amount displayed beside a product may be one instalment. It is not necessarily the full price.

## Delayed payment is still a commitment

A **repayment** is money paid back under the agreement. A repayment date creates a future use for money. Receiving the item now does not tell the customer how much money will be available on that date.

A payment calendar can make this visible. It can place work pay dates beside repayment dates and ordinary bills. The calendar is only a record. It cannot guarantee future work or remove an obligation.

Several purchases can also overlap. Each may seem small by itself, while their dates fall close together. To understand the total commitment, the question is about all the payments due, not just the newest purchase.

## Interest-free does not mean cost-free

**Interest** is a charge for using borrowed money over time. A **fee** is another charge. BNPL may include late, account, setup or payment-processing fees even when interest is not charged. A **late fee** is a charge for paying after the agreed time. Moneysmart also notes possible bank charges when scheduled payments cannot be covered. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/other-ways-to-borrow/buy-now-pay-later-services))

The cost question therefore has several parts. What is the item price? What is due immediately? What remains due later? Which charges depend on what happens? A total that excludes a possible fee is different from a guarantee that no fee can arise.

The agreement's wording matters more than an attractive headline. Two offers that both say “interest-free” can describe different fees, dates and conditions.

## Checks and missed payments

A **credit check** is a review of information about a person's borrowing history. A **credit report** is a record containing that information. Applications can trigger checks, and late or missed BNPL payments may be reported and affect future borrowing. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/other-ways-to-borrow/buy-now-pay-later-services))

The size of an individual purchase does not answer every question about the effect of missing a payment. Nor does approval prove that the payment dates will suit the customer's circumstances. Approval and personal planning answer different questions.

Australian BNPL arrangements fall within credit regulation. ASIC describes licensing requirements and transitional arrangements under the rules introduced in June 2025. This lesson does not treat BNPL as an unregulated payment method. ([ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/credit/buy-now-pay-later-credit-contracts-credit-licensing))

## Worked example

*These numbers are not real. They only show the idea.*

An imaginary purchase is split into four equal payments. In this example only, one missed payment creates the invented fee shown.

**Purchase**

- Paid at purchase: $100
- Three later payments together: $300
- Total item price: **$400**

**Example with a late fee**

- Item price: $400
- Invented late fee: $20
- Total paid: **$420**

Splitting the purchase changes payment timing. It does not reduce the item price. The fee changes the total cost; it is not a quoted provider charge.

## Things to think about

- How does paying the full price now differ from owing instalments later?
- What changes if the purchase waits until money has been saved?
- Could different purchases have payments due on the same day?

## Check your understanding

1. What is an instalment?
   - a) A discount on every purchase
   - b) One part of a total payment
   - c) Proof that no fees apply
2. True or false: Interest-free always means there are no costs beyond the item price.
3. Why is the full payment schedule more useful than just the first payment amount?

## Answers

1. **b) One part of a total payment.** Other payments may still be due.
2. **False.** Fees may apply.
3. **It shows the remaining amounts and dates.** These affect future available money.

## Glossary

- **Buy now pay later (BNPL):** Receiving goods or services and paying over time.
- **Instalment:** One part of a total payment.
- **Credit:** Receiving something now with an obligation to pay later.
- **Repayment:** Money paid back under an agreement.
- **Interest:** A charge for borrowed money over time.
- **Fee:** Another charge.
- **Late fee:** A charge for paying after the agreed time.
- **Credit check:** Review of borrowing information.
- **Credit report:** A record of borrowing information.
