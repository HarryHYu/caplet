# Opening and using a bank account

## What you will learn

- Tell everyday banking and saving accounts apart.
- Recognise common identity documents.
- Read account costs and payment details.

## Two different account purposes

A **transaction account** is an account for receiving money and making everyday payments. A **debit card** accesses money in an account when paying or taking out cash. Some transaction accounts charge monthly fees or fees for particular services. A **fee** is a charge for a service. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/banking/transaction-accounts-and-debit-cards))

A **savings account** is an account designed for money being saved. **Interest** is money a bank pays for holding savings, or money charged for borrowing. Savings accounts usually pay more interest than transaction accounts. Extra interest may depend on conditions, and an advertised introductory rate may end. Some savings accounts require a linked transaction account. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/banking/savings-accounts))

These descriptions explain purposes, not which account is suitable for every person. Someone may value easy access. Another person may want a separate place to record savings. The useful question is what the person needs the account to do.

A label alone does not explain every feature. The account information gives the details. Reading the conditions beside a headline is part of understanding the offer.

## What opening an account involves

**Identity documents** are records used to show who someone is. Banks must check identity, but the documents and methods they accept can differ. Examples include a passport, driver's licence, birth certificate and a recent bill addressed to the person. A bank can explain its requirements and possible alternatives when usual documents are unavailable. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/supporting-first-nations-people-with-money-matters/how-to-prove-your-identity))

This is not a universal document checklist. A document used by one family member may not answer every question for another person. Names written differently across documents can also make a question harder to resolve. It helps to distinguish the information requested from the evidence used to support it.

Questions about opening an account can be written in plain language: What information is needed? What evidence is accepted? How is that evidence submitted? Who can explain an unfamiliar term? These questions concern the process, rather than committing money to an account.

## Account details and access details

A **BSB**, short for Bank State Branch, is a six-digit number identifying a bank branch. An **account number** identifies a particular account when used with the BSB. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/glossary/bsb))

Those labels differ from a password. A password is used to sign in. A payment form and a sign-in screen therefore ask different questions. Understanding the purpose of each detail can help someone notice an unusual request.

Moneysmart warns about suspicious links and requests for personal information, and explains using strong passwords and additional sign-in checks. The fact that a message mentions a real bank does not establish that it came from that bank. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/online-safety/protect-yourself-from-scams))

## Reading costs fairly

An **account balance** is the money recorded in an account at a particular time. A fee reduces that balance. A comparison can separate regular charges from charges that depend on use.

Consider the same period and the same activities for each account. Comparing one month's charge with another account's yearly charge gives a misleading result. Likewise, a service someone never uses matters differently from one used often. This is arithmetic, not a recommendation.

## Worked example

*These numbers are not real. They only show the idea.*

Mina compares two made-up accounts for the same month and the same use.

**Account A costs**

- Monthly charge: $10
- Two service charges together: $10
- Total monthly costs: **$20**

**Account B costs**

- Monthly charge: $0
- Service charges for the same use: $10
- Total monthly costs: **$10**

**Difference**

- Account A costs minus Account B costs: **$10**

Account B costs less in this example. This does not establish which real account is best. Access, conditions and the actual fees would also matter.

## Things to think about

- Which services would be used regularly?
- Does extra interest depend on conditions that are easy to understand?

## Check your understanding

1. Which detail works with an account number to identify an Australian account?
   - a) A shopping list
   - b) A password hint
   - c) A BSB
2. True or false: Every bank must accept exactly the same identity documents.
3. Why compare account fees over the same period?

## Answers

1. **c) A BSB.** It identifies the branch associated with the account.
2. **False.** Accepted documents and methods can differ.
3. **It makes the comparison fair.** A monthly cost and a yearly cost are not directly comparable.

## Glossary

- **Transaction account:** An account for everyday payments and receipts.
- **Debit card:** A card accessing money in an account.
- **Savings account:** An account designed for saving money.
- **Interest:** Money paid on savings or charged for borrowing.
- **Fee:** A service charge.
- **Identity documents:** Records showing who someone is.
- **BSB:** A six-digit Bank State Branch number.
- **Account number:** The number identifying an account with its BSB.
- **Account balance:** Money recorded in an account at a particular time.
