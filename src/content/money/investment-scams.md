# Investment scams

## What you will learn

- Recognise claims that need independent checking.
- Explain what an Australian licence check can show.
- Know where official reporting and recovery guidance is available.

## A convincing story can still be false

An **investment** is something bought or funded with the aim of future income or growth. An **investment scam** is a dishonest offer that uses an investment story to take money or information.

Scammers can copy real businesses, create professional websites and invent positive reviews. They may use fake celebrity support or altered videos. A familiar face in an advertisement does not prove that person supports the offer. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/financial-scams/investment-scams))

A **return** is an investment's gain or loss over time. **Risk** means the chance of a worse outcome, including loss. Claims of unusually high returns with little risk, repeated contact and pressure to decide quickly are warning signs. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/check-and-report-scams/check-before-you-invest))

The warning is about evidence, not how confident a speaker sounds. A polite conversation and a clear-looking chart do not prove where money goes. Nor does a personal introduction answer that question.

## What a licence check means

The Australian Securities and Investments Commission, or ASIC, is the government regulator for companies and financial services. An **Australian financial services (AFS) licence** allows specified financial services. An **authorised representative** is permitted to act for a licence holder.

Moneysmart explains how to search ASIC's professional registers by business name or licence number. The relevant register shows licence holders or authorised representatives. Checking what the permission covers matters. Company registration alone is different from permission to provide financial services. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/check-and-report-scams/check-before-you-invest))

Scammers can copy another business's licence details. An independent comparison of the name, website and contact details helps test whether the person is really connected to that business. Even a genuine licence is not ASIC approval of an investment's safety or future result. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/check-and-report-scams/check-before-you-invest))

## Crypto and false account balances

**Crypto-assets** are digital assets that can be transferred electronically. A scam may demand payment in crypto or use a fake trading app. The app may show a growing balance that cannot be withdrawn. Crypto lost to scammers can be very difficult to recover. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/financial-scams/crypto-scams))

A number shown on a screen is a claim. It is not the same thing as money received. That distinction matters when someone is asked for another payment before getting their own money back.

## Worked example

*These numbers are not real. They only show the idea.*

A fictional app shows Sami a balance of $2,000 after a payment. Its operator then demands an extra “release fee”. This is an invented scam scenario, not a real investment.

**Money already sent**

- First payment: $1,000
- Money returned so far: $0
- Total sent and not returned: **$1,000**

**Further demand**

- Claimed release fee: $200
- Total sent if Sami pays again: **$1,200**

The displayed $2,000 is not counted as money received. Paying again would increase the amount at risk. It would not establish that the displayed balance exists.

## Reporting and getting help

Scamwatch is the reporting service run by the National Anti-Scam Centre, a government body that works against scams. Moneysmart directs people to report investment scams there, including when no money has been lost. Reports help identify patterns and warn others. Its reporting page links to the service. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/check-and-report-scams/report-an-investment-scam))

For money already sent, Moneysmart's urgent guidance includes stopping contact, contacting the bank promptly and protecting personal information. It also warns about people who demand a fee to recover lost money. Recovery is not promised. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/check-and-report-scams/report-an-investment-scam))

A report can describe what happened without having every answer. Messages, dates, payment details and the website address can help explain the sequence. Feeling embarrassed does not make a person responsible for someone else's deception.

## Things to think about

- Is the evidence independent of the person making the offer?
- Is a displayed balance being confused with money actually received?

## Check your understanding

1. What does company registration alone prove?
   - a) That all investments are safe
   - b) That the business has every financial permission
   - c) Neither of those claims
2. True or false: A genuine-looking celebrity video proves an offer is real.
3. Name the reporting service described in this lesson.

## Answers

1. **c) Neither of those claims.** Registration is separate from financial services permission.
2. **False.** Scammers can create false endorsements and altered videos.
3. **Scamwatch.** Moneysmart's reporting page links to this government reporting service.

## Glossary

- **Investment:** Something funded for possible future income or growth.
- **Investment scam:** A dishonest investment story used to take money or information.
- **Return:** An investment's gain or loss over time.
- **Risk:** The chance of a worse result, including loss.
- **Australian financial services (AFS) licence:** Permission to provide specified financial services.
- **Authorised representative:** Someone permitted to act for a licence holder.
- **Crypto-assets:** Digital assets that can be transferred electronically.
