# Tax when you start work

## What you will learn

- Explain why money is taken from pay towards tax.
- Understand the idea of a tax-free amount.
- Recognise the purpose of employment tax information.

## Paying towards yearly tax

**Income** is money earned or received. **Income tax** is tax charged on income. The ATO (Australian Taxation Office) is the government agency administering Australian tax. For employees, employers take tax from pay and send it to the ATO. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/work-and-tax/income-tax))

**Pay as you go (PAYG) withholding** is the system in which a payer takes tax from payments. A **payer** is the person or organisation making the payment. In a first job, that is usually the employer. The ATO's declaration instructions use these terms to explain employment tax information. ([ATO instructions](https://softwaredevelopers.ato.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-01/TFN_declaration_instructions_sample.pdf))

Money withheld is not an extra payment on top of the same final tax. It is money already paid towards the tax outcome. Understanding that distinction helps explain why a yearly calculation compares amounts already paid with the total due.

A single pay record is only part of the picture. Changes in hours or more than one source of income can make one period different from another. An amount taken out this week is not a promise that every future week will be the same.

## The tax-free threshold

The **tax-free threshold** is the amount of annual income on which an eligible person does not pay income tax. It is an annual concept, rather than a separate tax-free allowance for every payment. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/glossary/tax-free-threshold))

Moneysmart's resident income-tax table lists $18,200 as the nil-tax band. Its table is for Australian residents aged 18 and over, and it directs other groups to different rules. The amount is not a blanket rule for everyone living or working in Australia. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/work-and-tax/income-tax))

**Tax residency** means how someone is classified under tax rules. It is a question in the employment tax form explained below. The form also asks about claiming the threshold and certain study-related borrowing. These answers affect withholding. ([ATO instructions](https://softwaredevelopers.ato.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-01/TFN_declaration_instructions_sample.pdf))

For a young worker or new arrival, reading which group a table covers matters. A number copied from another person's form may not explain your own circumstances. This lesson explains the questions without selecting answers for a reader.

## The form at the start of work

A **tax file number (TFN)** is a personal number for tax administration. A **TFN declaration** gives a payer information used to calculate withholding. It is separate from applying for a TFN. The form includes personal details, employment basis and tax-status questions. ([ATO instructions](https://softwaredevelopers.ato.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-01/TFN_declaration_instructions_sample.pdf))

The linked PDF is an official sample used here to explain the form, not a form this lesson asks you to submit. An unfamiliar question can be discussed before an answer is given.

Accurate answers and clear records help make the calculation understandable. Guessing what an unfamiliar term means is different from confirming its meaning. A worker can understand why a question exists without already knowing which answer fits.

## Worked example

*These numbers are not real. They only show the idea.*

A student follows one fictional payment. The withheld amount is assumed and does not come from an Australian tax table.

**Pay before withholding**

- Work payment: $800
- Total before withholding: **$800**

**Tax withheld**

- Assumed amount sent towards tax: $100
- Total withheld: **$100**

**Money received**

- Payment minus withholding: **$700**

The $100 is recorded separately from the $700 received. The example shows movement of money, not the student's actual yearly tax.

## The yearly comparison

A **tax return** reports information used to work out a person's yearly tax position. Services Australia, the government agency delivering payments and public services, explains that the information helps establish whether enough tax was withheld. Outcomes can differ between years. ([Services Australia](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/what-to-do-tax-time?context=60271))

A **refund** returns an overpaid amount. A **tax bill** is an amount still owing. As a general calculation, paying too much or too little during a year can leave a difference to settle. Neither outcome follows simply from receiving a first payslip.

## Things to think about

- Does the information concern one payment or the whole year?
- Which words on an employment form need explanation?

## Check your understanding

1. What does a TFN declaration help a payer work out?
   - a) How much tax to withhold
   - b) Which groceries to buy
   - c) A guaranteed yearly refund
2. True or false: The tax-free threshold is a new allowance for each pay.
3. How is withholding different from money received by the worker?

## Answers

1. **a) How much tax to withhold.** The answers inform the calculation.
2. **False.** The threshold concerns annual income and eligibility.
3. **Withheld money goes towards tax.** Money received is what remains after amounts are taken out.

## Glossary

- **Income:** Money earned or received.
- **Income tax:** Tax charged on income.
- **PAYG withholding:** Taking tax from payments under the pay as you go system.
- **Payer:** The person or organisation making a payment.
- **Tax-free threshold:** Annual income amount on which an eligible person pays no income tax.
- **Tax residency:** Classification under tax rules.
- **TFN:** A personal number used for tax administration.
- **TFN declaration:** Information given to help a payer calculate withholding.
- **Tax return:** A report for working out yearly tax.
- **Refund:** An overpaid amount returned.
- **Tax bill:** Tax still owing.
