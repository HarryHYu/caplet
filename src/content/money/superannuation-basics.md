# Super: money for later in life

## What you will learn

- Explain why retirement money is separate from everyday money.
- Recognise employer payments and account costs.
- Understand where lost accounts can be found.

## What super is for

**Superannuation**, or super, is money saved for **retirement**, when someone stops working. A **super fund** manages and invests that money. An **investment** uses money with the aim of earning more money, but it can lose value. Most workers in Australia receive employer payments into super. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-super-works/superannuation))

A **contribution** is money added to a super account. Employer contributions come from the person or business employing a worker. A **balance** is the amount recorded in an account at a particular time.

Super can seem distant to someone starting a first job. Yet a work record can contain both spending money and money for later life. Understanding the difference explains why one figure might appear in a bank account and another in a fund account.

The amount added to an account is not necessarily the amount by which its balance grows. Costs and changes in investment values can affect the result. This makes a simple record of additions different from a complete account statement.

## Employer payments and account records

An employer payment, a fund receipt and a later balance are three separate pieces of information. The first describes money sent. The second describes money received. The third describes the whole amount held at a point in time.

This distinction helps when comparing records. A number without a date or label can be hard to interpret. A payment covering one period cannot be compared directly with a balance built up over several periods.

## Fees and other features

A **fee** is a charge for a service. Super funds can charge a dollar amount, a percentage of money held, or both. Moneysmart distinguishes costs for running the account and managing investments. It also explains that past investment results do not guarantee future results. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-super-works/choosing-a-super-fund))

A comparison therefore has more than one question. What period does the cost cover? Does it change with the balance? Are the investment options being compared similar? These questions help someone understand a comparison without deciding which fund to choose.

**Insurance** is an agreement that can pay money when certain covered events occur. Some super accounts include insurance, with costs taken from the account. Combining accounts can affect that cover, so it is not simply an arithmetic exercise. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-super-works/superannuation))

## Finding accounts

**Lost super** is super that a person has lost track of. Moneysmart explains that the money can still belong to someone after a move, a name change or a change in contact details. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-super-works/find-lost-super))

The ATO (Australian Taxation Office), the government agency administering tax and super matters, provides ways to find super. A **myGov account** gives access to linked government services. When linked to the ATO, it can show super accounts. Moneysmart also describes telephone and other search options. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-super-works/find-lost-super))

Finding an account and moving its money are different decisions. A search helps establish what exists. It does not establish that any particular change is suitable.

*These numbers are not real. They only show the idea.*

A practice super record begins with a balance and adds a fictional contribution. Investment changes, tax and insurance are excluded.

**Money recorded**

- Starting balance: $1,000
- Contribution received: $100
- Total before fees: **$1,100**

**Costs**

- Account fee: $10
- Total costs: **$10**

**Closing balance**

- Total before fees less costs: **$1,090**

The balance grew by less than the contribution because a fee was taken out. Real statements can contain more entries.

## Check your understanding

1. What is a contribution?
   - a) Money added to super
   - b) A password
   - c) A promise that investments cannot fall
2. True or false: Finding a lost account means its money must immediately be moved.
3. Name one reason a balance can change besides contributions.

## Answers

1. **a) Money added to super.** An employer payment is one type.
2. **False.** Finding and moving an account are separate steps.
3. **Fees or investment changes.** Either can affect the amount held.

## Glossary

- **Superannuation:** Money saved for retirement.
- **Retirement:** The stage when someone stops working.
- **Super fund:** An organisation managing and investing super.
- **Investment:** Money used to seek earnings, with a possibility of loss.
- **Contribution:** Money added to super.
- **Balance:** The amount held at a particular time.
- **Fee:** A service charge.
- **Insurance:** An agreement covering specified events.
- **Lost super:** Super a person has lost track of.
- **myGov account:** Access to linked government services.
