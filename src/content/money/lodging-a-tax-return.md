# Lodging your first tax return

## What you will learn

- Explain what a yearly tax report does.
- Recognise the online process and the usual deadline.
- Understand why the result may be a refund or a bill.

## The year being reported

A **tax return** is information reported to work out a person's yearly tax position. **Lodging** means formally submitting that information. A return is not the same thing as money paid back.

An **income year**, also called a financial year in this lesson, runs from 1 July to 30 June in Australia. Services Australia, the government agency delivering payments and public services, explains this period in its tax-time guidance. ([Services Australia](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/what-to-do-tax-time?context=60271))

A year on a document therefore needs careful reading. A calendar year starts in January, but this reporting period starts in July. Matching records to the correct period prevents money from one period being mixed with money from another.

The ATO (Australian Taxation Office) is the government agency administering tax. Whether someone needs to lodge depends on their circumstances. Services Australia explains that people who do not need to lodge generally need to notify the ATO. Having no form submitted is different from establishing that none is needed. ([Services Australia](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/what-to-do-tax-time?context=60271))

## How online lodging works

**myTax** is the ATO's free online tax-return service. A **myGov account** provides access to linked government services. Online lodging requires myGov linked to the ATO. For people lodging their own return, the usual deadline is 31 October after the income year ends. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/work-and-tax/lodging-a-tax-return))

**Pre-filled information** is information already added to the return from records supplied to the ATO. It still needs checking, and missing information needs adding. Moneysmart explains that much of it is available by late July. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/work-and-tax/lodging-a-tax-return))

A screen containing information is not proof that every detail is right. Comparing the reporting period, name of the payer and amount with personal records helps reveal what a figure represents. An empty field is not evidence that nothing belongs there.

A useful way to understand the process is to separate preparation, checking and submission. Preparation gathers records. Checking resolves missing or unclear information. Submission sends the completed return. Finishing the first stage does not finish the other two.

## Income, expenses and the outcome

**Income** is money earned or received. A **tax deduction** is an eligible amount that reduces income used to calculate tax. It does not usually mean receiving the entire expense back in cash. Moneysmart explains that work-related claims need a connection to earning income and supporting records. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/work-and-tax/lodging-a-tax-return))

A receipt can show that money was spent, but the reason for spending also matters. The existence of a receipt alone does not explain whether an expense meets a particular tax rule. This lesson does not classify a reader's expenses.

**Tax withheld** is money taken from payments towards tax during the year. A **refund** is an overpaid amount returned. A **tax bill** is an amount still owing. Comparing the amount already paid with the final amount due explains these possible outcomes.

## Worked example

*These numbers are not real. They only show the idea.*

A fictional completed calculation gives a final tax amount. This example does not calculate tax rates, deductions or other obligations.

**Tax already paid**

- Total withheld during the year: $1,000
- Total already paid: **$1,000**

**Final tax**

- Assumed final amount due: $800
- Total final tax: **$800**

**Difference**

- Already paid minus final tax: **$200**

In this simplified example, the difference is an overpayment. A final amount higher than the amount already paid would instead leave an amount owing. Neither result is guaranteed for a real person.

## Free help and clear questions

The ATO's **Tax Help program** offers free, confidential assistance for eligible people, using trained volunteers. Moneysmart describes online, telephone and in-person help. Eligibility needs checking against the current ATO information; this lesson does not promise access based on income alone. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/publications-and-resources/money-tips-in-other-languages/tax-in-australia))

A clear help request can name the step that is unclear: linking the account, understanding an entry, checking an expense or submitting the return. This makes it easier to get an explanation rather than guess.

## Things to think about

- Do the records cover the same income year?
- Which pre-filled entries have been checked against records?

## Check your understanding

1. What does lodging mean?
   - a) Receiving a guaranteed refund
   - b) Formally submitting the return
   - c) Deleting work records
2. True or false: Pre-filled information never needs checking.
3. Why is a tax return different from a tax refund?

## Answers

1. **b) Formally submitting the return.** Preparation alone is not submission.
2. **False.** It may be incomplete or incorrect.
3. **A return reports information; a refund returns an overpaid amount.** Lodging does not guarantee money back.

## Glossary

- **Tax return:** Information reported to work out yearly tax.
- **Lodging:** Formally submitting information.
- **Income year:** The Australian tax reporting year from 1 July to 30 June.
- **myTax:** The ATO's online tax-return service.
- **myGov account:** Access to linked government services.
- **Pre-filled information:** Details already added from supplied records.
- **Income:** Money earned or received.
- **Tax deduction:** An eligible reduction in income used to calculate tax.
- **Tax withheld:** Money taken from payments towards tax.
- **Refund:** An overpaid amount returned.
- **Tax bill:** An amount still owing.
- **Tax Help:** A free ATO assistance program for eligible people.
