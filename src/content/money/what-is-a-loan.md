# What is a loan?

## What you will learn

- Identify the parts of a borrowing agreement.
- Separate the borrowed amount from the total repaid.
- Explain why one advertised number cannot answer every question.

## Money now, payments later

A **loan** is money provided under an agreement to repay it. The **borrower** receives the money. The **lender** provides it. The **principal** is the amount originally borrowed. A **repayment** is money paid back under the agreement.

Borrowing changes when money is available. It can make money available today while committing future money to payments. It does not turn that future obligation into income. **Income** means money received from sources such as work.

ASIC, the Australian Securities and Investments Commission, is a government regulator for companies and financial services. Its consumer information directs readers to the costs of borrowing and the ability to meet repayments. ([ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/what-we-do/moneysmart-for-consumers))

## Understand the cost words

**Interest** is a charge for using borrowed money over time. An **interest rate** expresses that charge as a percentage for a stated period. A **fee** is a separate charge. A **loan term** is the agreed length of the loan.

A repayment can cover interest as well as some of the amount owed. Comparing only the original amount and one repayment leaves out the number of repayments. A small regular payment and a large number of payments can still produce a large total.

The **total amount repaid** includes the original borrowing and the borrowing costs included in the agreement. The **cost of borrowing** is the amount above the original money received. These are related, but they are not the same number.

## What a comparison rate means

A **comparison rate** combines interest and most fees into a percentage for an example loan. Moneysmart explains that it depends on assumptions about the amount and term, so it is a guide rather than an exact result for every borrower. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/loans/personal-loans))

For a fair comparison, the assumptions need to describe the same borrowing task. If one figure relates to a different amount or period, the difference may reflect those assumptions rather than a better offer.

A rate is also different from a dollar total. The rate helps describe price. The dollar total answers how much money the assumed agreement would require altogether. Neither number, by itself, proves that repayments fit a household's circumstances.

## Read estimates as estimates

Moneysmart's personal loan calculator estimates payments and timing. Its assumptions exclude some fees and do not assess whether someone can afford the displayed repayments. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/loans/personal-loan-calculator))

This distinction matters in classroom work. A correct calculation can still answer an incomplete question. If a fee has been left out, the arithmetic may be correct for the inputs while the total remains incomplete.

It is useful to read the words beside a calculator as part of the result. They explain what the result includes, what it leaves out and what must stay unchanged for the estimate to apply.

## Worked example

*These numbers are not real. They only show the idea.*

An invented agreement provides $1,000 and charges the amounts shown over its whole term. Assume no additional charges.

**Money received**

- Principal: $1,000
- Total received: **$1,000**

**Amounts repaid**

- Original principal returned: $1,000
- Total invented interest: $100
- Total invented fees: $50
- Total amount repaid: **$1,150**

**Borrowing cost**

- Interest and fees together: **$150**

The borrower receives $1,000 but returns $1,150. The $150 is the borrowing cost in this example. It is not a real loan quote.

## Things to think about

- What amount arrives, and how much must be paid in total?
- Are the repayment dates consistent with when income arrives?
- Which costs are excluded from an estimate?
- What would change if the agreement allowed the rate or fees to change?

## Check your understanding

1. What is the principal?
   - a) The original amount borrowed
   - b) Every fee added together
   - c) The name of the lender
2. True or false: A comparison rate gives an exact cost for every loan amount and term.
3. What is the cost of borrowing in the worked example?

## Answers

1. **a) The original amount borrowed.** Other costs are additional.
2. **False.** It is based on a stated example and assumptions.
3. **$150.** This is the invented interest and fees above the $1,000 received.

## Glossary

- **Loan:** Money provided under a repayment agreement.
- **Borrower:** The person receiving borrowed money.
- **Lender:** The person or organisation providing it.
- **Principal:** Original amount borrowed.
- **Repayment:** Money paid back.
- **Income:** Money received from sources such as work.
- **Interest:** A charge for borrowed money over time.
- **Interest rate:** A percentage charge for a period.
- **Fee:** A separate charge.
- **Loan term:** Agreed length of a loan.
- **Total amount repaid:** Principal plus included borrowing costs.
- **Cost of borrowing:** Costs above the original money received.
- **Comparison rate:** Interest and most fees expressed as a rate for an example loan.
