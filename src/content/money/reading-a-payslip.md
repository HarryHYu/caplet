# Reading a payslip

## What you will learn

- Identify the money earned and the money received.
- Explain why tax and retirement payments appear separately.
- Compare a pay record with hours worked and money received.

## Start with the labels

A **payslip** is a record explaining a payment for work. **Gross pay** is pay before tax and other amounts are taken out. **Net pay** is what remains after those amounts are taken out.

Moneysmart describes a payslip as showing the hourly amount earned, gross pay, net pay, tax taken out and super information. It helps explain why the amount earned differs from the amount received. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/student-life-and-money/starting-a-job))

An **hourly rate** is the amount paid for an hour of work. A **pay period** is the stretch of time a payment covers. Both are needed to understand a pay calculation. A figure is difficult to check if its unit or period is missing.

Reading a payslip is a sequence of questions. Whose record is it? What period does it cover? What work is included? What was taken out? What amount was paid? The aim is to follow the calculation, rather than look only at the final number.

## Tax taken out

**Tax withheld** is money taken from a payment and sent towards tax. **Income tax** is tax on income, which is money earned or received. The ATO (Australian Taxation Office) is the government agency administering tax. Employers take tax from employees' pay and send it to the ATO. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/work-and-tax/income-tax))

An **employer** is the person or business employing a worker. The employer's payment process connects gross pay, withheld tax and net pay. A subtraction explains the difference when tax is the only amount taken out.

If several amounts are taken out, each needs its own label. A total without details does not explain the reason for every part. This lesson does not decide whether a particular deduction is permitted.

The amount withheld in a single pay is not a complete explanation of someone's tax position for an entire year. A weekly record covers only that week's calculation, while a yearly review considers a longer period.

## Super is a separate payment

**Superannuation**, or super, is money saved for retirement, when someone stops working. A **super fund** manages that money. A **contribution** is an amount added to the fund. Moneysmart explains that employer super is generally paid in addition to pay and that eligibility conditions apply, including for workers under 18. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/student-life-and-money/starting-a-job))

Super information is therefore not simply another amount available in the worker's everyday bank account. A payslip and a fund record answer different questions. One describes the employment payment record. The other shows what the fund has received and holds.

## Worked example

*These numbers are not real. They only show the idea.*

A fictional payslip shows 20 hours at an invented hourly rate of $30. The tax and super figures are supplied assumptions, not legal calculations or minimum pay rates.

**Pay earned**

- 20 hours at $30: $600
- Gross pay: **$600**

**Amounts taken out**

- Assumed tax withheld: $50
- Total taken out: **$50**

**Pay received**

- Gross pay minus tax withheld: **$550**

**Separate super record**

- Assumed employer contribution: $70
- Total contribution shown: **$70**

The bank payment is $550. The separate super amount is not subtracted again from gross pay and is not added to everyday spending money.

## Connecting records

A **bank statement** records money moving into and out of an account. Workers commonly receive wages into their bank accounts, alongside a payslip explaining the payment. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/publications-and-resources/money-tips-in-other-languages/money-and-working-in-australia))

Comparing the net-pay line with a bank entry can show whether the amounts match. Comparing recorded hours with a work record answers a different question. A matching bank entry does not, by itself, prove the hours were correct.

A clear question about a possible mistake names the period and the line that seems wrong. “Which hours are included here?” is more precise than “This looks strange.” Keeping the original figures visible makes an explanation easier to follow.

## Things to think about

- Does the bank entry match net pay rather than gross pay?
- Which line would need explanation if recorded hours were different?

## Check your understanding

1. Which amount is before tax is taken out?
   - a) Net pay
   - b) Gross pay
   - c) Account fees
2. True or false: Employer super is always everyday spending money received with net pay.
3. In the example, why is net pay lower than gross pay?

## Answers

1. **b) Gross pay.** It is the amount before deductions.
2. **False.** Super is recorded separately for retirement.
3. **The assumed $50 tax amount was taken out.** $600 minus $50 leaves $550.

## Glossary

- **Payslip:** A record explaining a work payment.
- **Gross pay:** Pay before amounts are taken out.
- **Net pay:** Pay after amounts are taken out.
- **Hourly rate:** Pay for an hour of work.
- **Pay period:** The time a payment covers.
- **Tax withheld:** Money taken from pay towards tax.
- **Income tax:** Tax on income.
- **Employer:** A person or business employing a worker.
- **Superannuation:** Money saved for retirement.
- **Super fund:** An organisation managing super.
- **Contribution:** Money added to super.
- **Bank statement:** A record of account payments and receipts.
