# The cash rate and interest rates

## What you will learn

- Explain the central bank's role.
- Distinguish the official overnight rate from a household's own rate.
- Describe why a rate change can affect borrowers and savers differently.

## What the RBA does

The **Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA)** is Australia's central bank, the public institution responsible for national monetary policy. **Monetary policy** means decisions that influence interest rates and wider economic conditions. The RBA's Monetary Policy Board makes these decisions. Its goals include stable prices and full employment. ([RBA](https://www.rba.gov.au/education/resources/explainers/what-is-monetary-policy.html))

**Inflation** means a rise in the general level of household prices. **Price stability** means low, stable inflation. **Full employment** means as many people in jobs as can be maintained while inflation stays low and stable. It does not mean nobody is between jobs. ([RBA](https://www.rba.gov.au/education/resources/explainers/full-employment.html))

The RBA also issues Australia's banknotes and works on the safety and efficiency of the payments system. Its role is broader than one rate announcement. ([RBA](https://www.rba.gov.au/about-rba/))

## What the cash rate means

**Interest** is money paid for the use of money. An **interest rate** expresses that cost or payment as a percentage over a period. The **cash rate** is the market interest rate on overnight loans between financial institutions. The RBA sets a target for this rate. It strongly influences other interest rates. ([RBA](https://www.rba.gov.au/about-rba/))

A **loan** is money borrowed under an agreement to repay it. A **deposit** is money placed with a financial institution. Household loan and deposit rates are not automatically the cash rate. Competition, funding conditions and the risk of a loan also affect them. ([RBA](https://www.rba.gov.au/education/resources/explainers/the-transmission-of-monetary-policy.html))

This means two questions need separate answers. What did the RBA announce? What terms apply to a particular account or loan? A national announcement answers the first question, not every detail of the second.

## Borrowing, saving and spending

Higher borrowing costs can leave borrowers with less money for other spending. Higher deposit rates can increase savers' interest income. **Income** means money received. Lower rates can have the opposite effects. The effect depends on the person's borrowing and saving position. ([RBA](https://www.rba.gov.au/education/resources/explainers/the-transmission-of-monetary-policy.html))

A **household budget** is a plan for money coming in and going out. Looking at both sides helps explain why the same announcement can mean different things to different people. Someone may also borrow and save at the same time.

The RBA describes how changing rates influences activity and inflation. The effects take time, and their size is uncertain. A decision is not an instant change in every shop price. ([RBA](https://www.rba.gov.au/education/resources/explainers/the-transmission-of-monetary-policy.html))

## Reading a rate statement

A percentage needs a period and a base amount. Without these, it is incomplete information. “Per year” describes a different period from “per month”. The amount on which interest is calculated matters as well.

A payment also needs a label. **Principal** is the amount borrowed, apart from interest. A repayment can include principal and interest. Comparing a total repayment with an interest-only figure would mix different quantities.

The next example deliberately shows interest only. It keeps the borrowed amount unchanged so the arithmetic is visible. It does not model a real loan contract.

## Worked example

*These numbers are not real. They only show the idea.*

A fictional loan has an unchanged principal of $1,000 for a full year. Compare simple annual interest at two made-up rates. There are no fees, repayments or interest charged on interest.

**Earlier rate**

- Principal: $1,000
- Annual interest at 5%: **$50**

**Later rate**

- Principal: $1,000
- Annual interest at 6%: **$60**

**Difference**

- Extra annual interest: **$10**

The extra cost is $10 under these assumptions. It is not a statement of the current cash rate or an estimate of a real family's loan payment.

## Things to think about

- Is the figure a cash rate, an account rate or a total repayment?
- Does the description explain the time period and amount used?

## Check your understanding

1. What does the cash rate relate to?
   - a) Overnight loans between financial institutions
   - b) A fixed price for all bank accounts
   - c) The price of banknotes
2. True or false: Every household interest rate must equal the cash rate.
3. Why can a rate increase affect borrowers and savers differently?

## Answers

1. **a) Overnight loans between financial institutions.** The RBA sets a target for this rate.
2. **False.** Other factors also affect household rates.
3. **Borrowers may pay more interest while savers may receive more.** The effect depends on the person's position and account terms.

## Glossary

- **Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA):** Australia's central bank.
- **Monetary policy:** Decisions influencing interest rates and economic conditions.
- **Inflation:** A rise in the general level of consumer prices.
- **Price stability:** Low and stable inflation.
- **Full employment:** As many people in jobs as can be maintained with low, stable inflation.
- **Interest:** Money paid for the use of money.
- **Interest rate:** A percentage expressing interest over a stated period.
- **Cash rate:** The overnight lending rate between financial institutions.
- **Loan:** Money borrowed under an agreement to repay.
- **Deposit:** Money placed with a financial institution.
- **Income:** Money received.
- **Household budget:** A plan for money coming in and going out.
- **Principal:** The amount borrowed apart from interest.
