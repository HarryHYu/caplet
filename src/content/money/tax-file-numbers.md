# Your tax file number

## What you will learn

- Explain the purpose of a personal tax number.
- Identify where the application process begins.
- Tell a genuine need for information from an unexplained request.

## A number that identifies you

A **tax file number (TFN)** is a personal number issued for Australian tax administration. The ATO (Australian Taxation Office) is the government agency responsible for that administration. Employers and banks are among the organisations that use a TFN for tax purposes. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/glossary/tax-file-number-tfn))

A TFN is not a bank account. It cannot hold spending money. It is also not a password for an online account. These distinctions matter when several forms arrive together after someone starts work.

**Tax** is money collected by government under its rules. **Superannuation**, or super, is money saved for retirement. A TFN helps manage both tax and super. Moneysmart explains that the number is personal and needs secure handling. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/publications-and-resources/money-tips-in-other-languages/tax-in-australia))

Think about the difference between a name and a private identifier. Many people can have the same name. A personal number helps link records to the right person. That usefulness also makes an unexplained request for the number worth examining carefully.

## Applying through official information

An application for a TFN is free through the ATO. Services Australia, the government agency delivering payments and other public services, links to the official application from its supporting-documents guidance. ([Services Australia](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/supporting-documents-for-your-centrelink-claim?context=21816))

The application process starts with finding the official ATO instructions that fit the applicant. Reading the instructions is different from sending an application. It lets someone identify the relevant form and understand what information is requested before submitting personal details.

An application is also different from an employer's request for tax details. The first asks the government to issue a number. The second concerns using that number in work records. Filling in one document does not mean every other document has been completed.

## Giving the number for a clear purpose

An **employer** is the person or business employing a worker. Moneysmart explains that a new worker gives their TFN to their employer, which takes tax from pay and sends it to the ATO. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/publications-and-resources/money-tips-in-other-languages/tax-in-australia))

A bank may request a TFN for tax administration too. That does not mean a random caller claiming to be a bank has established a genuine need. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/glossary/tax-file-number-tfn))

The purpose, recipient and method of sharing information are separate questions. A real organisation's name can appear in a false message. An explanation of what a form does is more useful than pressure to complete it immediately.

## If information is misused

**Identity theft** means someone uses another person's information to pretend to be them. Warning signs can include unfamiliar charges, missing mail or messages about services the person does not use. Moneysmart describes contacting affected financial organisations and getting identity-support help. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/online-safety/identity-theft))

Services Australia's Scams and Identity Theft Helpdesk supports people whose myGov or related information has been exposed. **myGov** is an account used to access linked government services. The agency can arrange an interpreter when someone calls for help. ([Services Australia](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/scams-and-identity-theft?context=60271))

## Worked example

*These numbers are not real. They only show the idea.*

A learner separates money from identifying information in a practice record.

**Money received**

- Pay received into a bank account: $500
- Total money received: **$500**

**Money spent**

- Travel and food: $100
- Total spending: **$100**

**Remaining money**

- Pay received less spending: **$400**

The TFN is not part of this calculation. No real TFN belongs in a classroom money example.

- Who needs the information, and for what purpose?
- Which details in a form remain unclear?

## Check your understanding

1. Which organisation issues a TFN?
   - a) A supermarket
   - b) The ATO
   - c) Every employer separately
2. True or false: A TFN is a bank account holding money.
3. Explain one reason a TFN needs private handling.

## Answers

1. **b) The ATO.** It administers the number.
2. **False.** A TFN identifies a person for tax purposes.
3. **It can help someone misuse a person's identity.** It is personal information, not a public label.

## Glossary

- **Tax file number (TFN):** A personal number for tax administration.
- **Tax:** Money collected by government under its rules.
- **Superannuation:** Money saved for retirement.
- **Employer:** A person or business employing a worker.
- **Identity theft:** Using another person's information to pretend to be them.
- **myGov:** An account for linked government services.
