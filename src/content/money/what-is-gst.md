# What is GST?

## What you will learn

- Explain a tax included in many purchase prices.
- Distinguish a price before tax from a total including tax.
- Recognise the purpose of a tax invoice.

## A tax connected with purchases

**Goods and services tax (GST)** is a tax connected with many sales of goods and services. **Goods** are physical items. **Services** are activities people pay for. A **taxable sale** is a sale to which GST applies. For these sales, GST is 10% of the price before GST. Not every sale is taxable. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/work-and-tax/gst-calculator))

The Australian Taxation Office, or ATO, is the government agency that collects tax. **Tax** is money paid to government under the law. Government uses tax money for public services. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/publications-and-resources/money-tips-in-other-languages/tax-in-australia))

A business's GST position matters. Moneysmart explains that sales by a business registered or required to register are generally taxable unless an exception applies. This is why the word “GST” on a document needs to be read with the details of the sale. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/work-and-tax/gst-calculator))

## Included or added?

**GST-inclusive** means GST is already inside the stated total. **GST-exclusive** means GST has not been added. For a fully taxable sale, the GST component is one-eleventh of the inclusive price. It is not another 10% added to that same inclusive total. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/work-and-tax/gst-calculator))

These labels describe different stages of a calculation. Comparing an inclusive figure with an exclusive one would compare different amounts. Understanding the label prevents the same tax from being counted twice.

## Understanding the document

An **invoice** sets out a sale and the amount payable. A **tax invoice** is a sale document that includes information relevant to GST. A **receipt** records a purchase or payment. These labels are related, but they do not all answer exactly the same question.

A document may identify the seller, describe the purchase, show a date and separate the GST from the total. Reading those parts helps connect the document to the purchase.

## Glossary

- **Goods and services tax (GST):** A tax connected with many sales.
- **Goods:** Physical items.
- **Services:** Activities people pay for.
- **Taxable sale:** A sale to which GST applies.
- **Tax:** Money paid to government under the law.
- **GST-inclusive:** Already including GST.
- **GST-exclusive:** Before GST is added.
- **Invoice:** A document setting out a sale and amount payable.
- **Tax invoice:** A sale document with information relevant to GST.
- **Receipt:** A record of a purchase or payment.
