# Shares and funds

## What you will learn

- Explain owning part of a company.
- Distinguish owning a company share from owning part of a fund.
- Understand how charges change the amount an owner receives.

## Owning a share

A **share** is a small ownership part of a company. The **share market** is where buyers and sellers trade shares. In Australia, most share trading takes place on the Australian Securities Exchange, or ASX, a marketplace for these trades. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/shares/how-to-buy-and-sell-shares))

A company may pay a **dividend**, which is a payment to its shareholders from profits. Not every company pays dividends. Share prices can rise or fall. **Profit** means the amount left when costs are taken from money earned. A **capital gain** happens when a share is sold for more than its purchase price; a **capital loss** is the opposite. Costs also matter to the final result. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/shares/how-to-buy-and-sell-shares))

Owning a share does not mean owning the company's whole bank balance. It also does not mean the company promises to return the purchase price. The ownership part and the price someone else will pay are separate ideas.

## Owning part of a fund

A **managed fund** brings together money from many people and invests it according to a plan. A **fund manager** chooses or manages its investments. An **investment** is something bought or funded with the aim of future income or growth. **Income** is money received.

People own a part of the fund rather than directly owning its individual investments. Some funds pay **distributions**, meaning payments of income from the fund. The value of the fund can rise or fall. Different funds can own very different things. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/managed-funds-and-etfs/what-is-a-managed-fund))

The name “fund” describes an arrangement, not a single level of safety. To understand an arrangement, there are two useful questions: what does it own, and how are decisions made? An attractive name cannot replace those details.

## What makes an ETF different?

An **exchange traded fund (ETF)** is a managed fund whose units trade on a stock exchange. A **unit** is an ownership portion of the fund. Some ETFs follow an **index**, a measure tracking a selected group of investments. Others have managers who choose investments to meet a goal. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/managed-funds-and-etfs/exchange-traded-funds-etfs))

An ETF can hold many investments, but its contents may focus on one industry or country. Its name alone does not show how widely the money is spread. **Risk** means the chance of a worse result, including losing money. Risk depends on what the ETF owns and how it operates. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/managed-funds-and-etfs/exchange-traded-funds-etfs))

## Why fees matter

A **fee** is a charge for a service. **Brokerage** is the fee charged to arrange a purchase or sale. Shares may also involve account charges. Funds can have management charges, and ETF trades may involve brokerage. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/shares/how-to-buy-and-sell-shares), [Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/managed-funds-and-etfs/exchange-traded-funds-etfs))

**Return** means a gain or loss over a stated period. A figure before charges is not the amount left after them. Costs can apply even when the investment performs poorly.

A comparison needs a clear starting point, ending point and list of included costs. Different periods or missing charges can make two figures look more alike than they really are.

## Things to think about

- What does a fund own, and does its name make that clear?
- Which costs are included in a displayed result?

## Check your understanding

1. What does a person usually own when buying into a managed fund?
   - a) An ownership part of the fund
   - b) Every company in full
   - c) A guaranteed bank payment
2. True or false: Every ETF follows an index.
3. Why might a result before fees differ from the amount left for the owner?

## Answers

1. **a) An ownership part of the fund.** The underlying investments are held through the fund.
2. **False.** Some ETFs use active investment decisions.
3. **Fees reduce the amount left.** They are costs even if the investment loses value.

## Glossary

- **Share:** A small ownership part of a company.
- **Share market:** A marketplace where shares are traded.
- **Dividend:** A payment to shareholders from profits.
- **Profit:** Money earned less costs.
- **Capital gain or loss:** The difference between sale and purchase prices, before considering relevant costs.
- **Managed fund:** Money from many people invested under a shared plan.
- **Fund manager:** The person or business managing a fund's investments.
- **Investment:** Something funded or bought for possible future income or growth.
- **Income:** Money received.
- **Distribution:** Income paid out by a fund.
- **Exchange traded fund (ETF):** A managed fund traded on a stock exchange.
- **Unit:** An ownership portion of a fund.
- **Index:** A measure tracking a selected group of investments.
- **Risk:** The chance of a worse result, including loss.
- **Fee:** A service charge.
- **Brokerage:** A charge for arranging a trade.
- **Return:** Gain or loss over a stated period.
