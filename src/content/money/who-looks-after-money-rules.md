# Who looks after money rules in Australia?

## What you will learn

- Name four public agencies with different responsibilities.
- Match a money question to the kind of information an agency provides.

## ASIC: companies and financial services

The **Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC)** is the government regulator for companies, financial markets, financial services and consumer credit. A **regulator** is a public body that administers and enforces rules. **Credit** means borrowing under an agreement to repay. ASIC also makes company information available to the public. ([ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/our-role))

A question about a financial business's permission to provide services belongs to a different area from a question about a person's tax payment. Knowing this distinction helps narrow a search for official information.

## ATO: tax

The **Australian Taxation Office (ATO)** is the government agency that collects tax. **Tax** is money paid to government under the law. It helps fund public services, including schools, hospitals and transport. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/publications-and-resources/money-tips-in-other-languages/tax-in-australia))

Tax information can explain obligations and processes. A personal calculation can depend on facts about the person. A general page is therefore a starting point for understanding the question, rather than proof that every reader has the same result.

## RBA: the central bank

The **Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA)** is Australia's central bank. A **central bank** is a public institution with responsibility for national monetary conditions. The RBA sets monetary policy, issues banknotes and helps maintain reliable payment systems.

**Monetary policy** means decisions that influence interest rates and economic conditions. An **interest rate** is a percentage expressing the cost of borrowing or payment for saving over a period. RBA decisions affect these conditions across the economy. ([RBA](https://www.rba.gov.au/about-rba/))

## Services Australia: payments and services

**Services Australia** is the government agency delivering programs including Centrelink, Medicare, Child Support and myGov. **Centrelink** delivers government support payments and services. **Medicare** is Australia's public health care system. **Child Support** concerns arrangements to help pay children's costs after separation. **myGov** is an online way to access linked government services. ([Services Australia](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/who-we-are?context=22))

These names describe different functions. A government website address alone does not mean every agency can answer every money question. The topic, the agency's role and the relevant page all matter.

A clear enquiry can state what the document or payment is called, what is unclear and what information is needed. That is often more useful than describing the issue only as “a money problem”.

## Glossary

- **ASIC:** Australian Securities and Investments Commission.
- **Regulator:** A public body administering and enforcing rules.
- **Credit:** Borrowing under an agreement to repay.
- **ATO:** Australian Taxation Office.
- **Tax:** Money paid to government under the law.
- **RBA:** Reserve Bank of Australia.
- **Central bank:** A public institution responsible for national monetary conditions.
- **Monetary policy:** Decisions influencing interest rates and economic conditions.
- **Interest rate:** A percentage expressing interest over a period.
- **Services Australia:** The government agency delivering major public payments and services.
- **Centrelink:** Government support payments and services.
- **Medicare:** Australia's public health care system.
- **Child Support:** Arrangements for children's costs after separation.
- **myGov:** Online access to linked government services.
